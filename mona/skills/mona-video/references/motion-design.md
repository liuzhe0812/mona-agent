# Review the actual film

Use Huashu Motion's director, rhythm, craft and continuity methods. Whole-film
movement needs a subject that persists, a meaningful transformation, a clear
focus and time to understand its result. Type and camera movement support that
meaning; a list of entrance effects is not a storyboard.

Build the hardest shot first inside the confirmed global timeline. Check its
actual frames before expanding the film. Adapt continuous, montage or hybrid
rhythm to the content; narration-led explanation may need deliberate holds.
A segment boundary is not a reason to replace everything on screen.

Preview samples provide technical evidence only. For important spoken moments,
record what object actually changes and what the viewer can infer. Verify that
labels are readable at final size, identities and values remain traceable, the
subtitle area stays clear, and the last spoken idea has useful reading time.
Check across boundaries and seek forward/backward to the same time.

Actual pixel differences, motion signatures and no runtime errors do not prove
an explanation is right or attractive. Repair the cause; never add motion just
to satisfy a counter. Review full playback and audio when available, and report
when only sampled visual observation was possible.
