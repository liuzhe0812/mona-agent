# Style systems

Use this reference to turn vague requests such as “高级、科技、专业” into consistent, visible art direction. The source library mixes domains such as UI, architecture, documents, and characters with true visual styles; this guide reorganizes them into renderable systems.

Use the selected case's visual system or the caller's established direction first. This guide fills missing art direction; it does not replace a specific case with generic style words. Choose a dominant system and assign compatible secondary media explicit roles. The palette and material suggestions below are starting points, not restrictions on user-supplied art direction.

## Editorial minimal

**Visual grammar:** one dominant subject, asymmetric grid, large quiet areas, sharp hierarchy, restrained details.

**Surface:** matte paper or clean flat fields; crisp edges; subtle print grain if warmth is needed.

**Color and light:** two neutrals plus one accent; high legibility; broad soft light or flat graphic contrast.

**Useful for:** presentation heroes, report covers, premium posters, publishing visuals.

**Avoid:** generic corporate gradients, equal-sized cards, excessive icons, decorative lines, and several competing accents.

## Scientific atlas

**Visual grammar:** precise hierarchy, numbered modules, controlled cutaways, scale cues, legends, and short labels.

**Surface:** fine technical linework combined with carefully shaded specimens or spatial models; light paper texture.

**Color and light:** low-saturation base with functional color coding; neutral, even illumination.

**Useful for:** explainers, educational graphics, anatomy, systems, scientific scale diagrams.

**Avoid:** cyberpunk decoration, ornamental data, repeated modules with no scale difference, and paragraph-length annotations.

## Isometric systems illustration

**Visual grammar:** consistent isometric projection, modular environments, visible flows, clear foreground-to-background relationships.

**Surface:** clean vector or softly rendered 3D forms, rounded geometry, small human-scale details.

**Color and light:** controlled palette with one color per system; soft ambient shadows; no dramatic perspective.

**Useful for:** infrastructure, operations, urban systems, technology ecosystems.

**Avoid:** mixed viewing angles, tiny unreadable labels, ornamental complexity, and disconnected floating objects.

## Precision technical vector

**Visual grammar:** orthographic or cutaway views, uniform stroke logic, exact alignment, sparse annotations.

**Surface:** flat vector fills, fine outlines, exploded components, subtle depth only where needed.

**Color and light:** neutral base with functional accents; minimal gradients.

**Useful for:** product breakdowns, process diagrams, mechanical concepts, instructional graphics.

**Avoid:** photorealistic lighting, ambiguous component relations, decorative pseudo-measurements, and excessive labels.

## Premium technology

**Visual grammar:** large abstract or physical anchor, layered depth, smooth arcs or data fields, purposeful negative space.

**Surface:** dark glass, translucent acrylic, brushed metal, controlled luminous particles; physically coherent reflections.

**Color and light:** charcoal or midnight base, cool blue/teal light, one warm or electric accent; directional rim light.

**Useful for:** AI, computing, energy, security, enterprise technology visuals.

**Avoid:** neon circuitry clichés, random holographic dashboards, blue-purple gradient fog, and meaningless binary text.

## Product studio

**Visual grammar:** product dominates the frame; one clear viewing angle; props explain material, use, or flavor.

**Surface:** accurate package geometry and material response; clean sweep or purposeful set design.

**Color and light:** brand-led palette; large softbox key light, controlled rim light, grounded contact shadow.

**Useful for:** commerce, packaging, product campaigns, beauty, food and beverage.

**Avoid:** altered logos or package copy, duplicate products, unrelated props, floating objects without physical logic, and plastic-looking materials.

## Cinematic realism

**Visual grammar:** a decisive story moment, layered staging, motivated camera angle, readable silhouette.

**Surface:** natural skin and material detail, atmospheric depth, subtle lens behavior.

**Color and light:** motivated practical light, controlled contrast, coherent warm/cool relationship, restrained grade.

**Useful for:** narrative scenes, people at work, campaigns, historical reconstructions.

**Avoid:** empty spectacle, generic orange-and-teal grading, over-smoothed skin, impossible light sources, and background events unrelated to the story.

## Documentary photography

**Visual grammar:** observed moment, imperfect framing, plausible environment, small evidence-rich details.

**Surface:** natural texture, mild sensor or film grain, believable wear and mess.

**Color and light:** available light, realistic shadow direction, modest dynamic range.

**Useful for:** field work, daily life, street moments, credible report imagery.

**Avoid:** studio posing, pristine CGI surfaces, perfect symmetry, beauty retouching, and theatrical effects unsupported by the setting.

## Architectural calm

**Visual grammar:** one credible viewpoint, strong spatial planes, clear circulation, human-scale cues.

**Surface:** materially accurate timber, stone, glass, concrete, textile, and vegetation.

**Color and light:** daylight or one explicit time of day; coherent interior/exterior temperature; soft bounce light.

**Useful for:** interiors, buildings, public spaces, landscape architecture.

**Avoid:** warped verticals, impossible spans, mixed perspectives, furniture scale errors, and unmotivated glossy surfaces.

## Tactile editorial illustration

**Visual grammar:** simplified shapes with a strong focal metaphor, layered cut-paper depth or hand-made edges.

**Surface:** gouache, risograph, paper fibers, ink bleed, pencil, or screen-print texture.

**Color and light:** small palette, visibly mixed pigments, graphic shadow shapes rather than synthetic glow.

**Useful for:** conceptual articles, education, culture, human-centered topics.

**Avoid:** default glossy AI rendering, overly smooth gradients, random decorative doodles, and style words without a compositional idea.

## Refined ink and wash

**Visual grammar:** focal silhouette, expressive empty space, controlled overlap, rhythm from dark ink to pale wash.

**Surface:** dry-brush edges, diluted ink blooms, paper absorbency, sparse mineral or muted color accents.

**Color and light:** black, warm gray, paper white, and one restrained accent; atmospheric tonal transitions.

**Useful for:** cultural subjects, poetic posters, double exposure, historical or landscape imagery.

**Avoid:** fantasy-effect clutter, pasted-together scenery, generic calligraphy, faux parchment overload, and mixed historical motifs.

## Character production sheet

**Visual grammar:** repeated grid, fixed scale, consistent turnaround or action views, clean separation between poses.

**Surface:** controlled linework or neutral 3D clay/material render; identity details remain stable.

**Color and light:** neutral background, consistent lighting, limited palette tied to costume.

**Useful for:** character concepts, pose sheets, animation references, collectible designs.

**Avoid:** outfit or face drift, changing body proportions, crowded panels, decorative scenery, and inconsistent camera distance.

## Historical editorial

**Visual grammar:** period-specific subject and environment, clear artifact evidence, restrained museum or cinematic framing.

**Surface:** material cues appropriate to the period; archival print, fine painting, or grounded cinematic realism.

**Color and light:** palette derived from period materials and available light rather than generic “ancient gold.”

**Useful for:** historical scenes, timelines, museum visuals, classical culture.

**Avoid:** mixed dynasties or eras, modern fixtures, invented costume closures, fantasy architecture presented as fact, and random antique filters.

## Series anchors

For a slide deck, report, campaign, or sequence, keep five or six anchors unchanged across all prompts:

```text
Shared visual system: tactile editorial illustration; simplified geometric figures; warm ivory paper; deep navy and muted coral with small sage accents; broad flat shadows from upper left; subtle risograph grain; calm, intelligent tone; no text or logos.
```

Then vary only the scene, focal action, crop, and amount/location of negative space. Repeating a concise anchor is more reliable than paraphrasing the style on each image.
